A full-body MRI is a single imaging scan that uses magnetic fields and radio waves — not X-rays or radiation — to create detailed pictures from head to thigh, usually in under an hour. It can survey many organs at once, including some with no routine screening test, and can sometimes reveal signs of cancer and other conditions before symptoms appear.

No. A full-body MRI is not a substitute for standard, guideline-recommended screening such as mammograms or colonoscopy, and its long-term benefit as a general screening tool is still being studied. It can also flag findings that turn out to be harmless and need follow-up. That is why every scan we fund is meant to be reviewed with a licensed physician. See our Research & Sources.
