How it works

One pooled fund. A clear path from gift to grant.

Frontline Heroes Foundation raises donations into the First Responder MRI Fund. Eligible first responders apply through this website, and the foundation awards grant support based on published criteria and responsible grantmaking practices.

The process

How the Screening Fund works

1

Donors give.

Supporters contribute to the First Responder MRI Fund — one-time gifts, monthly gifts through the After the Call Club, and major gifts from companies and community groups.

2

Responders apply.

Eligible first responders submit a simple, privacy-conscious application online. No medical records, diagnosis details, Social Security numbers, or insurance IDs are collected in the public form.

3

We review.

Frontline Heroes Foundation reviews applications based on eligibility, service history, exposure risk, need, available funding, and program criteria.

4

Grants are awarded.

Approved applicants receive a grant toward a full-body MRI screening at an accredited imaging provider, to be reviewed with a licensed physician.

5

Responders get next-step support.

We help approved applicants understand the process and connect with appropriate healthcare resources.

How applications are evaluated

Every application is reviewed against the same criteria. Grants depend on available donations and responsible grantmaking practices — applying does not guarantee an award.

  • Eligibility as a current or former first responder
  • First responder service history
  • Occupational exposure risk
  • Financial or access need
  • Program criteria
  • Clinical appropriateness
  • Available donations and responsible grantmaking practices

What grants can support

One thing, done well: the full-body MRI

Every grant funds the same thing: a full-body MRI screening for an eligible first responder, at $2,000 per scan. A grant may cover:

  • A full-body MRI screening at an accredited imaging provider
  • Physician review of the scan and its results
  • Help scheduling and preparing for the MRI
  • Navigation to appropriate healthcare resources if follow-up is recommended
The appropriate screening path depends on age, risk factors, exposure history, family history, medical history, and current clinical guidance — decided with a licensed healthcare professional, not by the foundation.
